Our mission

Expanding access to advanced STEM education

LTT INC expands access to advanced education in science and technology for people facing economic barriers in Latin America, in high-impact STEM fields. Our goal is to help form the critical science and technology talent the Americas need, by removing the financial obstacles that keep capable students from completing advanced programs.

Artificial intelligence

Machine learning, data science and the computing skills behind applied AI.

Microelectronics & chip design

Analog, digital and mixed-signal integrated circuit design, layout and verification.

Robotics & related fields

Robotics, embedded systems and other technical disciplines connected to advanced industry.

What we fund

Scholarships and mentoring

Current program

Scholarships and micro-grants

  • Micro-grants of USD 500–1,000 per student per year.
  • Partial STEM scholarships of USD 1,500–2,500 per student per year.
  • Uses: tuition, training programs, certifications and academic development.
  • Mentoring and educational networking with volunteer professionals.
Planned for 2027 · subject to Board approval

Graduate scholarships

  • Master's scholarships of up to USD 12,000 for 12 months.
  • PhD scholarships of up to USD 12,000 per year, for up to four years, renewed annually based on academic progress.
  • Eligibility extended to U.S. residents facing economic barriers.

These programs are being designed and will only be offered once formally approved by our Board of Directors.

How recipients are selected

Scholars are chosen by an independent Scholarship Selection Committee using objective, neutral criteria:

  • Documented financial need
  • First-generation university students
  • Academic merit and technical potential
  • Eligible program of study and residence

Ethnicity, race, national origin, religion and sex are never conditions of eligibility. Donors may support a field of study but never choose individual recipients. Directors, officers, substantial donors and related parties are not eligible.

Governance & transparency

Accountable by design

  • Governed by a volunteer Board of Directors.
  • Conflict-of-interest policy adopted by the Board.
  • Independent Scholarship Selection Committee.
  • Sanctions (OFAC) screening of recipients.
  • Written scholarship agreements with academic progress reporting.
  • Annual information return (Form 990) filed with the IRS.
